## 1. Why this file exists

Stripe is a private company that discloses selectively. Every profitability and revenue figure in public circulation is a third-party estimate. This file separates facts from interpretation.

**The short version.** The case study's central thesis rests on four claims:

| Claim | Standing |
|---|---|
| Stripe is building open, non-exclusive protocol positions (ACP under Apache 2.0, Shared Payment Tokens, Tempo) | 🟢 **Well-evidenced** — first-party and verifiable |
| Agentic checkout has not yet converted; OpenAI retired Instant Checkout in March 2026 | 🟢 **Well-evidenced** — multiple independent sources |
| Stripe's merchant trust surface may be its weakest asset, and this matters strategically | 🟠 **Interpretation** — complaints are real, but the strategic weight is the author's argument |
| Risk-related signals are predictive enough for proactive warnings | 🔴 **Unverifiable from outside** — deferred to Phase 2 of the proposal |

## 4. Known weaknesses

1. **Complaint data cannot support a prevalence claim.** Argument rests on severity, not frequency.
2. **Partial defection is plausible but not measured.** Case does not depend on it being widespread.
3. **No primary research.** No interviews, usability testing, or telemetry.
4. **Proactive warnings depend on internal predictive capability.** Deferred to Phase 2.

## 5. Methodology

Research conducted via web search on 8 August 2026. Sources: Stripe newsroom, 2025 annual letter, tier-one business press, payments trade press, analyst comparisons, and public complaint aggregations. Every financial figure cross-checked against at least two sources where available. No primary-source interviews, product telemetry, or non-public documents were used.
